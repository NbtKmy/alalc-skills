# Capitalization

**Source:** ALA-LC Romanization Table: Japanese (2022), §3  
**Phase:** Phase 1 — Skill rules  
**Purpose:** Apply ALA-LC capitalization after the relevant reading and word division have been determined.

> **Unofficial restatement.** This file is an unofficial, operational summary of the ALA-LC Romanization Table: Japanese (2022). It is not the official table; the source document always takes precedence. See NOTICE.md at the repository root.

> Capitalization often depends on entity type and semantic status. The same lexical material can be capitalized differently when used as a proper name, a title, or a common noun.

## 1. General rule (§3)

Capitalize proper names according to the specific rules below.

**Never capitalize particles.**

If a proper-name case is not covered by the specific rules, standard English-language cataloging capitalization practice may be followed, but the ALA-LC table does not require it.

---

## 2. Personal names (§3.1)

Capitalize each word of a personal name. Particles within the name remain lowercase.

### Example

- 菅原孝標女 → `Sugawara no Takasue no Musume`

---

## 3. Titles and terms of address (§3.2)

Capitalize titles and terms of address when used as such.

Exception: a single character or kana such as `san`, `sama`, `chan`, `kun`, etc., when hyphenated after a personal name, remains lowercase after the hyphen. See also §6.9.2.3.

### Examples

| Japanese | Romanization |
|---|---|
| 弘法大師 | `Kōbō Daishi` |
| お菊さん | `Okiku-san` |
| 井上さん | `Inoue-san` |
| 内閣総理大臣田中角栄 | `Naikaku Sōri Daijin Tanaka Kakuei` |

When the same expression is a generic occupation rather than a title attached to a person, do not capitalize it:

- 内閣総理大臣 → `naikaku sōri daijin`

### Decision point

The Skill may need to determine whether an expression functions as a title/term of address or as a generic occupation.

---

## 4. Geographic names (§3.3)

Capitalize each word of a geographic name.

### Examples

| Japanese | Romanization |
|---|---|
| 横浜 | `Yokohama` |
| 日本列島 | `Nihon Rettō` |
| 有楽町 | `Yūraku-chō` |
| 太平洋 | `Taiheiyō` |
| 房総半島 | `Bōsō Hantō` |
| 東洋 | `Tōyō` |
| 富士山 | `Fujisan` |
| 淀川 | `Yodogawa` |

Word division and hyphenation for geographic names are handled more fully in §6.10.

---

## 5. Corporate and conference names (§3.4)

Capitalize each word of a corporate or conference name **except articles, prepositions, and conjunctions**.

### Examples

| Japanese | Romanization |
|---|---|
| 戦争を記録する会 | `Sensō o Kirokusuru Kai` |
| 国際仏教文化学術会議 | `Kokusai Bukkyō Bunka Gakujutsu Kaigi` |
| 日本陸軍 | `Nihon Rikugun` |
| 中央公論新社 | `Chūō Kōron Shinsha` |

Compare a title of a work:

- 中央公論 → `Chūō kōron` (journal title)

### Decision point

Entity classification matters. The same words may be capitalized differently depending on whether the expression is a corporate name or the title of a work.

---

## 6. Names of works (§3.5)

For names of works such as documents, publications, artworks, etc.:

1. Capitalize the **first word** of the title.
2. Capitalize any proper names contained within the title according to their own capitalization rules.
3. Other words remain lowercase unless another rule requires capitalization.

### Examples

| Japanese | Romanization |
|---|---|
| 徒然草 | `Tsurezuregusa` |
| 朝日新聞 | `Asahi shinbun` |
| 労働組合法 | `Rōdō kumiaihō` |
| 日本国有鉄道就業規則 | `Nihon Kokuyū Tetsudō shūgyō kisoku` |
| 蛍の光 | `Hotaru no hikari` |
| 富嶽三十六景 | `Fugaku sanjūrokkei` |

---

## 7. Historical and cultural events (§3.6.1)

Capitalize each word of the name of a historical or cultural event. Particles remain lowercase.

### Examples

| Japanese | Romanization |
|---|---|
| 第二次世界大戦 | `Dainiji Sekai Taisen` |
| 二・二六事件 | `Niniroku Jiken` |
| 明治維新史 | `Meiji Ishin shi` |
| 関ヶ原の戦い | `Sekigahara no Tatakai` |
| 祇園祭 | `Gionmatsuri` |
| 関東大震災 | `Kantō Daishinsai` |
| 福島原発事故 | `Fukushima Genpatsu Jiko` |

Note that an attached generic/common element may remain lowercase when the example shows it functioning outside the proper-name core, as in `Meiji Ishin shi`.

## 11. Derivatives of proper names: capitalize (§3.9.1)

A derivative of a proper name is generally capitalized when it is still functioning as a proper-name derivative rather than as an independent common noun.

### Examples

- `Nihonjin`
- `Amerikajin`
- `Kakyō`
- `Nihonhei`
- `Nihontō`
- `Nihonshu`
- `Nihonga`
- `Nihonshi`
- `Nihongo`
- `Eigo`
- `Nihongaku`
- `Kangaku`
- `Eibungaku`
- `Yōmeigaku`
- `Kirisutosha`
- `Kirishitan`
- `Bukkyōto`
- `Zensō`
- `Butsuga`
- `Zendera`

---

## 12. Derivatives of proper names: do not capitalize (§3.9.2)

Do not capitalize a derivative of a proper name when it has acquired an established, distinct **common-noun meaning**.

The source explicitly notes that no complete prescriptive rule can settle every case. General and long-continued usage must be considered.

### Examples

| Japanese | Romanization |
|---|---|
| 呉服 | `gofuku` |
| 瀬戸物 | `setomono` |
| アラビア数字 | `arabia sūji` |
| ローマ字 | `rōmaji` |
| ボストンバッグ | `bosuton baggu` |

### Decision point

This is a semantic and usage-based classification problem and is a strong candidate for AI or reference-source assistance in Phase 1.

---

## 13. Special cases with 和, 漢, 洋, etc. (§3.9.3)

Words containing characters such as `和`, `漢`, and `洋` are **not automatically proper names** merely because those characters may refer historically or semantically to Japanese, Chinese, or Western concepts.

Capitalize only when the expression specifically refers to a nation, dynasty, language, or national/ethnic entity.

### 13.1 Not referring to a nation (§3.9.3.1)

| Japanese | Romanization |
|---|---|
| 和室 | `washitsu` |
| 洋酒 | `yōshu` |

### 13.2 Referring to a nation, dynasty, language, or national/ethnic entity (§3.9.3.2)

| Japanese | Romanization | Note |
|---|---|---|
| 和寇 | `Wakō` | `和` refers to an attributed national/ethnic affiliation |
| 和英辞典 | `Wa-Ei jiten` | `和` refers to the Japanese language |
| 倭人 | `Wajin` | national/ethnic entity |
| 倭国 | `Wakoku` | country/entity |

Additional contrasts:

| Japanese | Romanization |
|---|---|
| 漢字 | `kanji` |
| 漢方 | `kanpō` |
| 漢薬 | `kan'yaku` |
| 漢 (Han dynasty) | `Kan` |

## 14. Suggested Phase 1 decision procedure

```text
Is the expression a proper name or does it contain one?
├─ no → normally lowercase unless another rule says otherwise
└─ yes
   ↓
What kind of entity/expression is it?
├─ personal name → capitalize each name word; particles lowercase
├─ title/term of address → capitalize title; hyphenated -san etc. lowercase
├─ geographic name → capitalize each word
├─ corporate/conference name → capitalize words except articles/prepositions/conjunctions
├─ name of work → capitalize first word + embedded proper names
├─ historical/cultural event → capitalize each word
├─ historical/cultural period → capitalize first word
├─ structure/road/street → capitalize name
├─ religion/sect → capitalize name
└─ derivative of proper name → determine whether it remains proper or has become a common noun
```

## 15. Automation notes

### Likely automatable after entity type is known

- Apply capitalization pattern for personal names.
- Apply title-of-work first-word capitalization.
- Keep particles lowercase.
- Apply capitalization to a known corporate/conference/geographic/religious entity class.

### May require contextual or external-reference judgment

- Whether an expression is a proper name at all.
- Title/term of address versus generic occupation.
- Corporate name versus title of a work.
- Event name versus descriptive phrase.
- Proper-name derivative versus lexicalized common noun.
- Whether `和`, `漢`, `洋`, etc. refer specifically to a nation, dynasty, language, or national/ethnic entity in context.

### Open questions for Phase 1

- Which entity classifications can be obtained reliably from UniDic, and which require an NER/LLM layer?
- Should capitalization be applied only after word division is finalized?
- How should ambiguous proper/common-noun status be represented in the intermediate result?
